# Scoring rubric

Five dimensions, scored independently, each 0-3, with `no evidence` as a distinct value
that is **not** a zero. Score every dimension before forming an overall view; the overall
is derived, never the starting point.

The rubric exists so that two screeners reach comparable rankings on the same pile, and
so that a hiring manager can see which dimension a decision turned on.

## The dimensions

### 1. Evidence of shipped work (weight: highest)

Something that exists and can be examined: a repository, a document, a policy, a
published piece, a product, a number they moved with the mechanism attached.

- **3** — Multiple artifacts, and their own contribution is legible within them.
- **2** — One solid artifact, clearly theirs.
- **1** — Artifacts described but not shown, with specifics that suggest they are real.
- **0** — Claims only, and generic ones.
- **no evidence** — The application format gave them nowhere to put any. Common with
  bare ATS forms; this is a defect in your funnel, not in the candidate.

### 2. Depth on the core task

Not "has the title" — can they describe the actual work with the tradeoffs intact?

- **3** — Describes a real decision, the alternative rejected, and what it cost.
- **2** — Describes the work concretely, no tradeoff.
- **1** — Describes the domain, not their work in it.
- **0** — Buzzwords standing in for the task.

A useful test: could this sentence have been written by someone who only watched? "Led
customer experience transformation" passes that test, which is why it scores 1 at best.

### 3. Finishing

The single most predictive and least claimed signal. Look for things that were completed,
maintained, handed over, or run for a long time. Started-and-abandoned patterns matter:
five half-built projects is a 1, one boring finished thing is a 3.

### 4. Range against the specific bar

Score against the must-haves you wrote down, not against an imagined ideal. A candidate
missing one must-have with a 3 in evidence is often a better call than one who nominally
meets all five with 1s across the board.

### 5. Signal of self-correction

Do they describe a mistake, a reversal, or something they changed their mind about? Rare,
and it separates people who have owned outcomes from people who have narrated them. Score
0 for absence rather than penalising — most applications never get the chance to show it.

## Handling `no evidence`

`no evidence` is a statement about the application, not the person. Never average it in
as a zero. Two rules:

- If a dimension is `no evidence` for **most** of the pile, the funnel is not asking for
  it. Report that as a finding and screen on the rest.
- If a dimension is `no evidence` for **one** candidate on a must-have, that is the
  question to ask them, not a reason to cut them — unless the pile is deep enough that
  you are choosing between evidenced candidates anyway. Say which situation applied.

## Deriving the ranking

1. Sort by dimension 1, then 3, then 2. Evidence and finishing are the two that survive
   contact with the job.
2. Apply the bar: a must-have scored 0 (not `no evidence`) drops a candidate below every
   candidate who clears all must-haves, regardless of totals.
3. Break remaining ties with dimension 5, then with what a single interview question
   could resolve — and say what that question is.
4. Never present a total without the per-dimension scores. A composite hides which
   dimension the decision turned on, which is the only part a hiring manager can argue
   with.

## Things that are never dimensions

School, employer prestige, title seniority, years of experience as a number, writing
polish outside writing roles, formatting, photo, name, location beyond a stated
requirement, employment gaps, hobbies, "culture fit", "passion", or enthusiasm expressed
in the cover letter. Several of these are protected-characteristic proxies; the rest are
weak predictors that crowd out strong ones.

Years of experience deserves a specific note: it is a proxy for dimensions 1-3, and
whenever you have those directly, the proxy adds nothing and imports age bias. Use it only
where a stated legal or contractual minimum exists.
