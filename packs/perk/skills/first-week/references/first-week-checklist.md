# First-week checklist

Two lists. The first is the one people skip and then apologise for. The second is the one
that makes the difference and costs nothing.

Treat both as living: every question a new person had to ask belongs in list two, and every
thing that was not ready belongs in list one with an owner.

## List one: before day one (verify, do not assume)

Each item needs a named owner and a confirmed-done state, not an assumption.

**Access**
- Laptop or machine, shipped and tracked, with an arrival date before day one.
- Email + SSO, and a **test login actually performed** — not just an account created.
- The three to five tools the role uses daily. Not everything; the daily ones.
- Repo, drive, or file access at the level the role needs, no more.
- Any physical access: building, desk, badge, keys.

**Paperwork** (official HR path only, never collected ad hoc)
- Contract signed, payroll set up, tax and bank details submitted.
- Right-to-work / eligibility handled by whoever is legally responsible.
- Benefits enrolment window and its deadline stated.

**People**
- Buddy named, told they are it, and asked to reach out **before** day one.
- Manager 1:1 on day one, in the calendar.
- The three check-ins (day 2, day 5, day 30) booked now, with their distinct questions.

**Day-one message**, sent the Friday before: start time, where to go or which link, who to
look for, what to bring, what the first day looks like, and a named person to contact if
anything fails to work. Include list two.

## List two: the questions nobody asks

Answer these in writing before anyone has to ask. Fill in your own answers; the value is
that they are written, not that they are interesting.

**The room**
- Where are the toilets, water, coffee, and the quiet space.
- Is there a kitchen, and is anything in the fridge communal.
- What do people do for lunch, and does anyone eat together.
- Is the office badge needed to get back in from outside.

**The rhythm**
- Which meetings are optional. Say it in the invite, not just here.
- What time do people actually start and stop. Not policy, practice.
- Is camera-on expected. Is it fine to eat on a call.
- What does an ordinary day look like for someone in this role.

**The channels**
- Which channel is real incidents, which is chat, which is nobody-reads-it.
  A channel map with "post here / do not post here" beats a channel list.
- Chat or email for a question, and what response time is normal for each.
- What every acronym in the channel names means. All of them.
- Is it fine to DM someone senior directly, or is that a channel thing.

**The work**
- Whose review is required before something ships, and whose is a courtesy.
- What breaks often and is known. Naming this early saves a week of self-doubt.
- Does anyone read the wiki, and which pages are current versus archaeology.
- What is the thing everyone complains about, so it is not a surprise.
- Who to ask when the tool you were told to use does not work.

**The unspoken**
- Is it fine to say "I do not know" in a meeting. (Say yes and then mean it.)
- Is it fine to leave a call early, decline a meeting, or block focus time.
- What counts as an emergency worth interrupting someone for.
- How do expenses work, and what does not need pre-approval.

## Feed the fixes back

After each day-5 check-in, capture the answer **verbatim** and add whatever they had to ask
about to list two. One line per hire is enough. An onboarding that does not gain a line per
hire is being re-run, not run — and the questions are always specific enough that guessing
them in advance does not work.
