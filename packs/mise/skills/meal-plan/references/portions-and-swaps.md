# Portions, shelf life and the swap table

Lookup material for `meal-plan`. Everything here is a PLANNING default. Actual storage times
depend on the fridge, the packaging and the date on the pack, and the date on the pack wins.

## Portion planning, per adult, per meal

| Component | Plan for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Dried rice or grains | 60 to 75 g | Roughly triples cooked |
| Dried pasta | 75 to 100 g | The single most over-served item |
| Potatoes | 200 to 250 g | More if it is the whole starch |
| Meat or fish, boneless | 120 to 150 g | Less when a pulse carries the dish too |
| Meat on the bone | 200 to 250 g | A roast chicken feeds 4 and leaves a second meal |
| Pulses, dried | 60 to 75 g | Doubles as the protein; cheapest line on the list |
| Pulses, tinned | Half to one tin | Drained weight is about 60 percent of the tin |
| Vegetables | 200 to 300 g | Split across two colours, not one |
| Leaves | 40 to 50 g | Wilts fastest of anything in the plan |
| Cheese, as flavour | 20 to 30 g | As the main, 80 to 100 g |
| Eggs, as the main | 2 to 3 | The cheapest rescue meal in the house |

Children take roughly half to two-thirds of an adult portion, and want it more often. Cook
the adult amount and keep a plain component aside rather than cooking twice.

## Order things get used, by how fast they die

**Two days**: leaves, soft herbs, berries, fresh fish, opened cream, mince once opened.
**Three to five days**: broccoli, courgette, mushrooms, cooked leftovers, opened yoghurt,
milk after opening, cooked rice (chilled fast, eaten within a day for safety).
**One to two weeks**: carrots, cabbage, peppers, celery, hard cheese, eggs, citrus.
**A month or more**: potatoes and onions in the dark, apples in the fridge, root vegetables.
**Pantry**: pulses, grains, tins, frozen anything.

Plan the first two meals of the week from the top of that list. Plan the end of the week from
the bottom of it. That single ordering removes most food waste without any extra effort.

## The swap table

Use when the plan meets a missing ingredient, a fussy eater or a bad day.

| Instead of | Use | Adjust |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh spinach | Frozen spinach | Squeeze it dry, add later |
| Fresh herbs | Dried, at a third of the amount | Add early, not at the end |
| Cream | Yoghurt or milk with a spoon of flour | Off the heat, or it splits |
| Buttermilk | Milk with a squeeze of lemon | Stand 10 minutes |
| Fresh tomatoes, cooked | Tinned | Cook longer to lose the tinny edge |
| Chicken breast | Thighs | Cheaper, more forgiving, longer cooking |
| Mince | Lentils, or half and half | Add extra seasoning and something umami |
| Stock | Water plus a bay leaf and salt | Season at the end, not the start |
| Wine in a braise | Water plus a splash of vinegar | Small splash |
| Rice | Any grain in the cupboard | Cooking time changes, taste it |
| A whole recipe, on a bad day | Eggs, toast, a tin, a bag of frozen veg | This is a plan, not a failure |

## Batch and reuse patterns

- **One roast, three meals**: roast, then sandwiches or a salad, then a stock and soup from
  the bones. The third meal is nearly free.
- **One base, two dinners**: a large pan of onion, carrot and celery becomes a ragu on
  Monday and a soup or a shepherd's pie on Wednesday.
- **Double the pulse, halve the effort**: cook twice the beans, half for tonight, half for a
  salad or a spread later in the week.
- **Cook once, freeze in portions**: soups, ragu, curry, stew. Label with the date, or the
  freezer becomes an archive.
- **Thursday is leftovers.** Any weekly plan with seven distinct cooked dinners is a plan
  that fails on a weekday, and it always fails on the same weekday.

## Shopping list, in aisle order

Produce, then bakery, then chilled dairy, then meat and fish, then frozen, then tins and
dry goods, then household. Write it in that order and one lap of the shop finishes it.
Non-negotiables at the top, nice-to-haves at the bottom so the budget cut is obvious. Check
the fridge and the cupboard BEFORE writing the list, not from memory: duplicate purchases
are the largest single source of household food waste, and they happen because the list was
written in another room.
